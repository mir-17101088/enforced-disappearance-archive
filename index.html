Case File

Three feet by four feet

Solitary confinement fits a human life into about twelve square feet: floor enough to sit, to crouch, or to lie folded against the wall. A fixed toilet takes one corner. For up to twenty-two hours a day, this is the entire world.

One of fourteen

Pull back and the cell is one of fourteen, sealed along a single controlled corridor. Each door faces a wall, not another person. Whatever contact remains passes through the officers who work the block.

Hidden inside a working base

Keep pulling back and the wing all but disappears. Around it runs the RAB Intelligence compound: armories, offices, a field, a common cell. The isolation unit is one quiet corner, easy to walk past and never see.

The Archive

Inside the Architecture of Secret Detention

An evidence-based archive of the secret detention and torture sites run by Bangladesh’s police, paramilitary, and intelligence agencies between 2009 and 2024 — reconstructed from survivor testimony and the findings of the Commission of Inquiry on Enforced Disappearances.

For fifteen years, people across Bangladesh were taken from streets, homes, and workplaces by men who never gave their names, held in rooms that officially did not exist, and returned — when they were returned at all — under orders to say nothing. When the Awami League government fell in August 2024, the Commission of Inquiry on Enforced Disappearances began documenting what those rooms had been. What it found was not a series of isolated abuses but the functioning of a system: a network of cells inside police headquarters, battalion compounds, intelligence facilities, and unmarked “safe houses,” each with its own routines of interrogation and torture.

This archive maps that network, agency by agency. Some of these sites still stand as they were. Others were repainted, retiled, or demolished in the weeks after the government fell — an attempt to erase the evidence that is itself part of the record.

The Network

A Single Machinery of Disappearance

Six agencies carried out the disappearances — but rarely alone. A person seized by one force was passed to the next: questioned by military intelligence, held in a battalion cell, moved to a shared torture centre. This plate traces who worked with whom, drawn from a subset of cases documented by the Commission of Inquiry on Enforced Disappearances.
